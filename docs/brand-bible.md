## 4. Brand Voice Guide

### Tone (5 Adjectives)

1. **Assured** -- we speak with quiet confidence, never hype
2. **Warm** -- approachable without being casual
3. **Precise** -- every claim is specific and substantiated
4. **Elevated** -- the language of a boutique, not a warehouse
5. **Unhurried** -- no urgency tactics, no countdown timers, no scarcity pressure

### 5 Things the Voice DOES

1. **Cites wavelengths, clinical studies, and specific outcomes** -- "630nm red light stimulates collagen synthesis" not "amazing anti-aging benefits"
2. **Treats the customer as already intelligent** -- assumes they have done some research and respects that
3. **Uses sensory, experiential language** -- describes how a session feels, not just what it does
4. **Acknowledges limitations honestly** -- "results typically visible after 4-6 weeks of consistent use" rather than overnight miracle claims
5. **Writes in short, rhythmic sentences** -- editorial in feel, scannable in practice

### 5 Things the Voice NEVER Does

1. **Never uses fear-based marketing** -- no "your skin is aging faster than you think" language
2. **Never uses ALL CAPS, excessive exclamation marks, or emoji clusters** in primary copy
3. **Never makes unsubstantiated medical claims** -- no "cures," "heals," or "reverses" without clinical backing
4. **Never talks down to the customer** -- no "Did you know...?" or condescending educational tone
5. **Never mimics Amazon-seller copywriting** -- no keyword stuffing, no "BEST SELLER #1 RATED" language

### Color Usage Rules

- **60/30/10 ratio:** 60% Warm Linen, 30% Midnight Ink, 10% Burnished Gold
- Gold is reserved exclusively for interactive elements and key brand moments
- Deep Infrared appears sparingly to reinforce the product category without overwhelming
- Never place Burnished Gold text on Warm Linen background (insufficient contrast)

### Typography

| Role | Font | Weight | Fallback |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| **Headings (H1-H3)** | **Cormorant Garamond** | Medium (500) & SemiBold (600) | Georgia, serif |
| **Body Copy** | **Inter** | Regular (400) & Medium (500) | system-ui, sans-serif |
| **Accent / Labels** | **Inter** | SemiBold (600), ALL CAPS, 1.5px letter-spacing | system-ui, sans-serif |
| **Monospace (specs)** | **JetBrains Mono** | Regular (400) | monospace |

**Rationale:** Cormorant Garamond is an open-source serif that carries editorial authority -- it says "magazine feature," not "medical device manual." Inter for body provides clean, highly legible reading at any size with excellent screen rendering. Both are available free via Google Fonts.

### Type Scale (Desktop)

```
H1:  48px / 56px line-height / Cormorant Garamond SemiBold
H2:  36px / 44px line-height / Cormorant Garamond Medium
H3:  24px / 32px line-height / Cormorant Garamond Medium
Body: 16px / 26px line-height / Inter Regular
Small: 14px / 22px line-height / Inter Regular
Label: 12px / 16px line-height / Inter SemiBold, uppercase, tracked
```

### Photography Style

**Art Direction: "Clinical Calm"**

- **Lighting:** Soft, diffused natural light with warm undertones. No harsh studio flash. Golden hour and overcast daylight preferred.
- **Color Temperature:** Warm (3500-4500K). Images should feel like they belong on the same page as the Warm Linen background.
- **Composition:** Generous negative space. Products shot on textured natural surfaces (linen, travertine, light wood). No cluttered flat-lays.
- **Human subjects:** Diverse, 25-40 age range. Photographed mid-ritual (eyes closed, relaxed posture, device in use). No "before/after" comparison grids.
- **Product shots:** 45-degree hero angles. One product per frame. Shadow direction consistent across all images (bottom-right at 30 degrees).
- **Post-processing:** Minimal retouching. Slight desaturation (-10-15%). Subtle grain overlay for tactile warmth. No heavy filters or neon glows.
- **Absolutely avoid:** Stock-photo smiling-at-camera poses, clinical/medical aesthetics (white coats, sterile rooms), neon LED glow photography that looks like gaming equipment.

### Primary Persona: "Maya Chen"

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Age** | 32 |
| **Location** | Austin, TX (or similar: Denver, Nashville, Brooklyn, LA) |
| **Occupation** | Senior Product Designer at a SaaS company |

**Values:**
- Optimization without obsession -- wants to feel good, not gamify her body
- Sustainability and ingredient/material transparency
- Investing in fewer, better things over accumulating cheap alternatives
- Evidence-based wellness -- skeptical of trends that lack research
- Time efficiency -- will pay a premium to save 30 minutes

**Pain Points:**
- Overwhelmed by the sheer volume of LED devices on Amazon with fake reviews and inconsistent quality
- Does not trust brands that use influencer hype without clinical data
- Has been burned by wellness products that overpromise (bought a cheap red light panel that did nothing)
- Wants guidance on which device actually fits her concern (acne scars vs. fine lines vs. inflammation) without reading 40 comparison articles
- Fears looking like she is wearing "medical equipment" -- aesthetics matter

**Where She Currently Shops:**
- **Skincare:** Drunk Elephant, Paula's Choice, The Ordinary (for basics)
- **Wellness devices:** Has browsed Therabody, HigherDose, and CurrentBody but hasn't committed
- **General lifestyle:** Aesop, Le Labo, Parachute Home, Mejuri
- **Discovers products via:** Instagram Explore, editorial roundups (The Strategist, Wirecutter), Reddit r/SkincareAddiction, friend recommendations

**Buying Triggers:**
- A well-designed product page that shows clinical evidence without being boring
- Real customer reviews from people who describe their specific skin concern
- A clear recommendation engine ("This device is right for you because...")
- Free shipping and a generous return window (she needs to feel the device before committing)

## 7. Content Pillars

### Pillar 1: "The Science Shelf"
Educational content that earns trust through specificity. Wavelength breakdowns, clinical study summaries, dermatologist Q&As, myth-busting. This is the pillar that separates us from every Amazon listing.

**Example posts:**
- "630nm vs 660nm vs 850nm: What each wavelength actually does to your skin (with citations)"
- Carousel: "5 claims red light therapy brands make that the research does not support"
- Video: A board-certified dermatologist reviews our top 3 devices

### Pillar 2: "The Ritual"
Lifestyle content showing how devices integrate into real routines. Morning light sessions, evening wind-down setups, pairing with skincare products. Aspirational but achievable.

**Example posts:**
- "My 12-minute evening reset: cleanser, serum, mask, read" (photo series)
- Reel: Setting up a red light session with ambient lighting and a playlist
- "How three of our customers built light therapy into their week" (UGC feature)

### Pillar 3: "Curated, Not Crowded"
Product curation content explaining why we carry what we carry and what we rejected. Transparency as a trust mechanism. Reinforces our editorial authority.

**Example posts:**
- "We tested 40 LED masks. We carry 4. Here is why."
- "What we look for before adding a device to our store" (ingredient-label-style breakdown)
- Comparison guides that are genuinely balanced, including honest downsides

### Pillar 4: "Skin Diary"
Real results documented over time. Not before/after clickbait but honest 30/60/90-day journals from real customers and team members. Raw, long-form, and specific.

**Example posts:**
- "Week 6 update: my hormonal acne scars under 630nm red light" (photo journal)
- Monthly check-in series with 3 customers tracking different concerns
- "What I wish I knew before starting red light therapy" (team member essay)

### Pillar 5: "The Drop"
New product launches, restocks, and limited collaborations. Treat product releases like editorial events, not sales announcements.

**Example posts:**
- "Arriving Friday: the device we have been waiting 8 months to carry"
- Behind-the-scenes testing footage of new arrivals
- Waitlist-first access for newsletter subscribers

## 8. Elevator Pitch

Novara is a curated online destination for clinical-grade LED and red light therapy devices, built for the wellness-literate consumer who has outgrown Amazon but is underserved by the current market. While incumbents either sell commodity panels with no guidance or bundle devices into broad wellness empires, we focus exclusively on light therapy with the editorial rigor of a Wirecutter review, the visual refinement of Aesop, and the trust mechanics of a board-certified recommendation. Our customers are affluent professionals aged 25-40 who spend $150-$600 on devices they will use daily -- and they need a brand that respects both their intelligence and their time. We are building the definitive light therapy brand for the next decade of at-home skincare technology.

### Brand-by-Brand Differentiation

| Dimension | **Novara (Us)** | **CurrentBody** | **Solawave** | **HigherDose** | **Amazon Sellers** |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|--------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Core model** | Curated marketplace with editorial authority | Retailer + own brand hybrid | Single-brand DTC (own devices) | Wellness lifestyle brand (own devices) | Open marketplace |
| **Product range** | 15-25 best-in-class devices, third-party sourced | 100+ SKUs across many brands | Narrow (5-10 own SKUs) | Narrow (infrared-focused own SKUs) | Thousands, unfiltered |
| **Trust mechanism** | Clinical citations, derm partnerships, transparent rejection criteria | Brand recognition, awards | Influencer marketing, press awards | Celebrity endorsements, wellness community | Star ratings, review volume |
| **Price positioning** | $80-$600 (mid-premium to premium) | $30-$800 (full spectrum) | $50-$250 (accessible premium) | $200-$1,000+ (premium) | $15-$500 (race to bottom) |
| **Visual identity** | Editorial luxury (Aesop meets Wirecutter) | Clean clinical (medical-tech) | Bright, youthful, social-first | Earthy, aspirational, spa-like | None / inconsistent |
| **Weakness we exploit** | N/A | Overwhelming selection, no strong POV on what is best | Limited to own devices, cannot recommend competitors | Light therapy is secondary to broader wellness play | Zero curation, trust deficit, counterfeit risk |
| **Our advantage over them** | N/A | We are opinionated; they are a department store | We carry the best from any manufacturer, including theirs | We are laser-focused on light therapy with clinical depth | We are the antidote to decision paralysis and fake reviews |

### Strategic White Space

The gap we occupy: **a curated, editorially driven, clinically rigorous, visually premium store that sells exclusively light therapy devices from multiple manufacturers -- functioning as both a retailer and a trusted authority.**

No current player combines all four of these attributes:
1. Multi-brand curation (not locked to own products)
2. Clinical depth (wavelength data, study citations, derm validation)
3. Premium visual identity (not clinical, not cluttered)
4. Narrow category focus (light therapy only, not general wellness)

## 10. Implementation Quick Reference

### Immediate Design Deliverables Needed

1. **Wordmark logo** in Cormorant Garamond SemiBold, lockup with optional icon
2. **Favicon and social avatar** derived from first letter or abstract light motif
3. **Color-applied component library** (buttons, cards, badges, form fields) in Figma
4. **Photography moodboard** with 15-20 reference images matching the "Clinical Calm" direction
5. **Email template** (header, product feature, editorial) using the type scale above
6. **Social templates** (Instagram grid, Stories, carousel) at brand-consistent specs
7. **Packaging insert card** (for shipped orders) -- matte stock, Burnished Gold foil accent

### Brand File Formats to Produce

- Logo: SVG, PNG (transparent, dark and light versions), favicon ICO
- Color palette: ASE (Adobe Swatch Exchange) + Tailwind config values
- Typography: Google Fonts import snippet + CSS custom properties
- Photography: Lightroom preset or Capture One style matching the described treatment
