- B: Not all red light is the same (here's why it matters)

**Preview text:** A two-minute read that'll save you from buying the wrong device.

**Body:**

Hi {{first_name}},

If you've started looking at LED devices, you've probably noticed that wavelength numbers are everywhere. 630nm. 660nm. 850nm. Sometimes all on the same product page with no explanation.

Here's the short version.

**630nm (visible red):** Primarily targets the surface layers of skin. Research supports its role in stimulating collagen production and improving skin texture. This is the wavelength most studied for fine lines and overall skin tone.

**660nm (deeper red):** Penetrates slightly further. Clinical trials show benefits for wound healing, reducing inflammation, and supporting cellular repair. Often the best starting point for acne scarring and post-inflammatory redness.

**850nm (near-infrared, invisible):** Goes beyond the skin into muscle and joint tissue. Used in sports recovery and deeper tissue repair. You won't see this light with your eyes, but your cells respond to it.

The takeaway: a device that only offers one wavelength isn't necessarily worse. It depends entirely on what you're trying to address.

A 630nm-focused LED mask is excellent for someone concerned with skin texture and fine lines. Adding 850nm makes sense if you also want recovery benefits or have deeper concerns like joint stiffness.

If you want to see how we evaluate devices across these wavelengths, our curation page explains exactly what makes the cut and what doesn't.

Your device should have arrived by now. If it hasn't, reply to this email and I'll look into it immediately.

If it has, let's make your first session count.

**Your first-session checklist:**

**Step 1: Charge fully before first use.** Even if the device turns on out of the box, a full charge ensures consistent light output during your entire session. Most masks take 1-2 hours.

**Step 2: Clean the device.** Wipe the inner surface (the side that touches your skin) with a soft, damp cloth. No harsh cleaners -- just water or a gentle alcohol wipe.

**Step 3: Prep your skin.** Wash your face with your regular cleanser. Pat dry. If you use a serum, apply a thin layer. Avoid anything thick or occlusive.

**Step 4: Position and start.** Place the mask comfortably on your face. Adjust the straps so it sits snugly without pressing. Start the device and set a timer for 10 minutes.

**Step 5: Relax. Genuinely.**

Close your eyes. The red light is safe but there's no reason to stare at it. Some people listen to a podcast. Some just breathe. This is your time.

**What to expect:** Nothing dramatic after one session. Your skin might feel slightly warm and look temporarily flushed -- that's increased circulation, which is exactly what we want. Visible changes in texture and tone typically begin appearing around weeks 3-4 with consistent daily use.
